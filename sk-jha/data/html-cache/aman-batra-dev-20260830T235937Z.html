    Recently I watched a video on "Conway's Game of Life". My first thought was why would anyone need that, and what could be its actual application. I researched a little and found out it has applications in simulating AI, computational and mathematical problems. This introduced me to cellular automata. In simpler words cellular automata are some defined rules being performed on cells in a grid shaped world. State of each cell is affected by its neighbouring cells. You define some rules during initialization and let the evolution unfold. This sparked an idea for me. Could I achieve a procedural generated environment using cellular automata? The goal was to create a whole level from just a single cell, no predefined maps, no outlines, no blueprints, nothing. And it turned out pretty well, not only the generated levels have proper collisions but also their size can be adjusted using parameters giving a control over random chaos.

    The rules for this automata are pretty simple and straightforward:

    Let S be the state of a cell:
    1. S-> Empty
    2. S-> Generate a Wall Cell
    3. S-> Generate a Floor Cell

    Depending upon the state of its neighbours, a cell makes a decision by selecting the most viable rule that fits the situation and triggers the rest of its neighbours who don't have any cell yet. Those neighbours parallelly and individually makes decision depending upon their neighbours and trigger the inactive neighbours on completion. Chain continues until a closed loop is formed.

      I'm excited to announce the release of my latest project, "Ignorance", now available on Itch.io. This isn't just another horror game; it's an experiment born from my curiosity about player autonomy and perception.
      In Ignorance, there are no guides, objectives, markers, or enemies. The menu provides all necessary controls, but beyond that, you're on your own. Players must rely on their intellect and awareness to discern the game's purpose. Every interaction and choice influences the outcome, leading to multiple possible endings. The environment offers subtle hints, but it's up to you to observe and interpret them. Remember, ignorance can be fatal.
